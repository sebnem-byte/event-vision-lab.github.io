## Continuous-time signal

At pixel $\mathbf{x}=(x,y)$, let $I(\mathbf{x},t)>0$ be brightness. Define log brightness as

$$L(\mathbf{x},t)=\log I(\mathbf{x},t).$$

Let $L_{\mathrm{ref}}$ be the reference log intensity after previous events. An ideal event occurs when the accumulated deviation crosses a threshold:

$$L(\mathbf{x},t_k)-L_{\mathrm{ref}}(\mathbf{x})=p_k C,\quad p_k\in\{-1,+1\}.$$

After the event, the reference level can be advanced by $p_k C$ in the ideal model. Actual sensor implementations may behave differently, especially under noise and rapid illumination changes.

## Why logarithmic brightness?

For two brightness levels $I_1$ and $I_2$,

$$\Delta L=\log I_2-\log I_1=\log\left(\frac{I_2}{I_1}\right).$$

So the ideal contrast rule is sensitive to **relative brightness change**, rather than a fixed absolute increment.

## Mini exercise

A pixel's intensity doubles. Compute $\Delta L$. For $C=0.2$, estimate the number of ideal positive threshold crossings if the reference starts at the initial brightness and no countervailing changes occur.
